Waking up every morning and following a fixed schedule has never been my thing. Why? I asked myself this many times. Why can't I be like normal, disciplined people who are happy following a schedule?

In search of an answer, I started delving into books, AI-based Q&A sessions, therapy, spirituality, philosophy, neurobiology and even Vedic astrology. In the end, when I turned 30 this year, I gave in to that *why* and moved on to embrace a *how*.

How can this restlessness be transformed into something that brings peace to my unsettled heart? How can everything I have learned be written in a way that others can read and find some calm for themselves? My own peace lies, somehow, in being a good listener and a storyteller.

The learning curve my fate has put me through in these past 29 years, along with the old gods and the new ones, has given me so many stories to tell. As if that weren't enough, the cat inside me went out to learn more about other fields, which gave me even more to write about. Thankfully, curiosity did not kill that cat. It only made her more learned in the facts of life, and in how to do things by thirty, hopefully.

So, starting today, I have decided to do more than write about the amalgamation of science, philosophy and literature. I will set up a personal, experiential experiment here: one essay a day, on a topic that comes to my mind at random. It might be a life lesson, or a story I went through, told in a way that lets readers learn something of their own.

At a TEDx talk back at my institution, I heard an eminent, learned man say:

> *Kahani likhne se daro nahi. Likho. Agar achhi nahi lagi, toh badal dena, kyunki kahani **tumhari** hai.*  
> Don't be afraid to write the story. Write it. If you don't like it, change it, because the story is yours.

And trust me, *kahani* here does not only mean a story. It means something much bigger. It means life. :)
