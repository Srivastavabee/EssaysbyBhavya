Right now, as you read this, billions of cells inside your body are working without your permission. Your heart is beating. Your neurons are firing. Proteins are being built. Memories are forming.

And somehow, the very thing trying to understand biology is itself a product of biology. That’s what makes this subject different. Biology isn’t just another chapter in a textbook. It’s the story of life studying itself.

Most students meet biology through definitions, diagrams, and exams. Somewhere along the way, they forget to be amazed. But biology begins with a simple question:

## How does life work?

Every chapter is an attempt to answer it. Every experiment starts with curiosity. Every discovery begins with someone noticing something others ignored.

The best students aren’t those who memorise the most. They’re the ones who keep asking why.

> Why does a seed wake up after months underground?  
> Why does a leaf turn toward sunlight?  
> Why does a single cell become an entire organism?

Biology rewards curiosity. So the next time you open a textbook, don’t just look for answers. Look for questions.

Because biology was never meant to be memorised. It was meant to be explored.
